# Credit and credit scores

Credit is borrowed money that must be repaid, usually with interest. How it is
managed is recorded and affects the cost and availability of future borrowing.

## Credit utilisation

Credit utilisation is the share of available credit currently in use:

    utilisation = balance owed / total credit limit

A card with a 100,000 limit carrying a 30,000 balance is at 30% utilisation.

Keeping utilisation **below 30%** is the usual guidance, and lower is generally
better. High utilisation suggests dependence on borrowing, and it is one of the
larger factors in most scoring models.

Two details are easy to miss. Utilisation is normally measured on the statement
date, not at month end — so a balance cleared after the statement is generated
can still be reported as high. And it is assessed both per card and across all
cards combined.

## Credit scores

A credit score summarises borrowing history into a single number, typically
between 300 and 900 in India, and lenders use it to price and approve credit.
The main inputs, roughly in order of weight:

- **Payment history.** Whether past dues were paid on time. The single largest
  factor. One missed payment can affect a score for years.
- **Credit utilisation.** As above.
- **Age of credit history.** Longer histories are viewed more favourably, which
  is why closing an old card can lower a score.
- **Credit mix.** Some variety across secured and unsecured credit.
- **Recent enquiries.** Several applications in a short period suggest
  difficulty obtaining credit.

## Paying in full versus the minimum

Paying a credit card in full by the due date usually incurs no interest. Paying
only the minimum — often around 5% of the balance — is expensive: card interest
is typically in the region of 30-45% a year, and the interest-free period is
usually lost on new purchases until the balance is cleared entirely.

Minimum payments are structured so that a balance can persist for years while
most of each payment goes to interest rather than principal.

## Common misunderstandings

- **Checking your own score does not lower it.** Your own check is a soft
  enquiry; only a lender's application check is a hard enquiry.
- **Carrying a balance does not build credit.** Paying in full on time builds
  credit. Carrying a balance only creates interest.
- **Closing unused cards is not automatically helpful.** It reduces total
  available credit, which raises utilisation, and can shorten credit history.
- **No credit history is not the same as good credit.** Someone who has never
  borrowed may find it harder to borrow than someone with a modest good record.
