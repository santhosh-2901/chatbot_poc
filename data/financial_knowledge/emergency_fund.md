# Emergency funds

An emergency fund is money set aside specifically for unexpected expenses:
medical costs, urgent repairs, sudden travel, or a period without income. Its
purpose is to absorb a shock without borrowing at high interest or selling
long-term investments at a bad time.

## What counts as an emergency

An emergency is unexpected, necessary and urgent. A broken boiler qualifies. A
holiday, a phone upgrade or a festival purchase does not, however desirable —
those are planned expenses, and planning for them separately is what keeps the
emergency fund intact.

## How much to hold

The common guideline is **three to six months of essential expenses** — not
three to six months of income, and not of total spending. What matters is the
cost of continuing to live: rent, utilities, food, transport, insurance, minimum
debt payments.

Where within that range depends on how stable income is and how many people
depend on it:

- **Three months** suits stable salaried income, dual-income households, or
  someone with few dependants and marketable skills.
- **Six months or more** suits variable or commission-based income, freelance or
  contract work, a single-income household, or anyone supporting dependants.

Someone spending 40,000 a month on essentials is therefore looking at a target
of roughly 120,000 to 240,000.

## Where to keep it

The fund must be available within a day or two and must not fall in value. That
means a savings account, a sweep-in deposit, or a liquid fund — not equities,
not long-tenure fixed deposits with penalties, and not anything whose value
moves with the market. Accepting a lower return is the point: this money buys
certainty, not growth.

Keeping it in a separate account from day-to-day spending matters more than it
sounds. Money that shares an account with routine spending tends to be spent.

## Building one

Building an emergency fund from zero can feel slow enough to be discouraging.
Two things help. First, set an initial milestone of one month of essentials —
reachable in a few months, and enough to cover most common shocks. Second,
automate a transfer on payday so it does not depend on remembering.

If there is high-interest debt, such as a credit card balance, the usual order
is to build a small buffer of about one month first, then clear that debt, then
return to the full three-to-six-month target. The interest saved outweighs the
interest earned.

## After using it

An emergency fund is meant to be spent when an emergency arrives — using it is
not a failure. Rebuilding it becomes the priority afterwards, ahead of
discretionary spending, until it returns to target.
